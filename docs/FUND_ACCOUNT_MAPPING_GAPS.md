# Funds to QuickBooks accounts: gaps and how they are closed

Owner priority 3a (2026-10-08): "Mapping of funds to accounts, with treasurer approval and 2FA: what are the gaps? Close them."

**What "the mapping" is.** It decides which QuickBooks account each kind of money posts to. Today it is three things:

| Part | Where it lives | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Account roles ("purposes") | `app.qbo_account_mappings` (one row per organization and role) | The income account of each **fund** (general donations, bolis, sponsorships, construction, Pathshala, jeevdaya, events, membership dues, store giving, other) and the other accounts: payment clearing, merchant fees, bank, undeposited funds, store sales, gift packing, sales tax, pledges receivable, stock clearing, pledge write-offs |
| A giving fund's class | `app.funds.qbo_class_id` | The QuickBooks class that keeps a restricted fund's money apart (construction, jeevdaya, deva dravya ...) |
| A bank account's QuickBooks account | `app.bank_accounts.qbo_account_id` | Which QuickBooks bank register a deposit or a Zelle/ACH line lands in |

"Fund" in this document means the stream of money (giving fund, bolis, dues, events, store and so on). Which fund a payment belongs to is the kind of its campaign; a pledge with no campaign is placed by its source (a membership fee is dues, a Pathshala fee is Pathshala, an event commitment is events, a boli is bolis).

**What is built and what was never proven.** Connecting, pulling the chart of accounts, the mapping with the treasurer's approval and 2FA, the test post and cash-basis posting are built, but the QuickBooks integration has only ever run against local mock services. Nothing below has been proven against a real Intuit sandbox company (that needs the owner's Intuit app and keys, backlog B59).

## What is deliberately not changed

- **Setup is unchanged.** Before the mapping has ever been approved, the treasurer (`accounting.manage`) chooses accounts and classes alone and approves the whole mapping with a fresh 2FA check, exactly as before (as in 0597, "the first time a payee is set is unchanged"). Those choices are now recorded in the history too.
- Approving the whole mapping again (after a basis change) is still one person: it does not change any account.
- A confirmed change keeps the go-live approvals (the mapping and the test post): posting continues with the new account (owner question 1).
- Nothing already posted is re-posted or adjusted.
- Old member-app builds are not affected (no member function changes).

## Questions for the owner

1. A confirmed change keeps the test post's approval, so posting goes on. Alternative: every change stops posting until a new test post is run and approved.
2. Should a restricted fund without a QuickBooks class block posting (today: a warning)?
3. Should a giving fund (Deva Dravya, Jiv Daya ...) or a campaign be able to have its own income account, ahead of its campaign kind? (Not built; the old campaign-level account is no longer used because nobody could approve it.)
4. Should the **first** approval of the whole mapping also need a second person?
5. Connecting a different QuickBooks company is still one person (with 2FA). Money cannot post to the new company until a second person confirms the new accounts, but should the switch itself need a second person?
6. An organization with one holder of `giving.approve` cannot change its mapping once it is in use (the same as 0597's payee changes). JSH needs a second holder.

## Only provable against a real Intuit sandbox (needs the owner's Intuit app and keys)

- That QuickBooks accepts the documents built with the new resolution (an item per income account, classes switched on, the bank register of a deposit).
- That a re-send under the same request id returns the first answer (Intuit's idempotency window).
- That account and class ids of two companies collide in practice (the guard assumes they can).
